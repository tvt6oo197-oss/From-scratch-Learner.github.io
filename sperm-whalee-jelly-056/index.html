Inside the experiment

Two jellies share one skin. The outer cast is clear, set a little firm, and holds its shape against gravity only because the tetrahedral cage beneath it is still remembering how it was poured. The inner layer is milk: it fills the belly, follows a slow wave along the flank, and sinks out of sight beneath the head where the jaw takes over.

The head is the loud part. Section after section the superellipse flattens from a round tube toward a square, so the skull arrives as a blunt box with a rounded face — the shape a sperm whale uses to sound, and the shape that makes a jelly read as a whale at all.

Thirty sugar teeth stand up from the lower jaw into the clear blue. Behind them, about halfway down the body, a squid is still lit: a warm core, photophore beads along every arm, all of it filtered through the jelly it is suspended in until the light arrives at the table the colour of deep water.

Blow a spout and the head dips as it exhales. Seventy-two drops leave the blowhole along its live axis, bounce off the flank, roll, and settle. Pick one up and throw it. It will find the floor again.
